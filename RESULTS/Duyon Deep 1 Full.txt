I. INTRODUCTION

a. Summary

This report provides the results of special core analysis for rotary [MSCT] sidewall cores from Well Duyong Deep-1, from the interval 3184.0-3598.5 m.

The well was drilled by PETRONAS CARIGALI Sdn. Bhd [PCSB] in Block PM12, offshore Peninsular Malaysia.

The analyses were undertaken and report prepared by UZMA ENGINEERING Sdn. Bhd. [Uzma] in partnership with PT. GEOSERVICES, Geological & Laboratory Services Division [PTG].

b. Objectives

The object of the study was to carry out the following analyses:

• Formation resistivity factor at net overburden [NOB] conditions
• Formation resistivity index by continuous injection at NOB conditions
• Cation exchange capacity
• Air-brine capillary pressure by centrifuge at NOB conditions
• Mercury injection capillary pressure to maximum of 2,000 psi

c. Samples & Analyses

Six [6] rotary sidewall cores were received for routine core analysis and rock description [petrography, XRD and SEM] and the results are presented in separate reports. Four [4] of the samples were then selected, by PCSB, for special core analysis, as listed in Figures 1-2.

II. SPECIAL CORE ANALYSIS

a. Introduction

Results are provided for the following on the four [4] samples selected for analysis:

• Formation factor at NOB conditions.
• Resistivity index by continuous injection at NOB conditions.
• Cation exchange capacity.
• Air-brine drainage capillary pressure by centrifuge at NOB conditions.
• Mercury injection drainage capillary pressure.

b. Results

• Formation Resistivity Factor & Resistivity Index at Net Overburden Conditions:
    Tables 1, 2, Graphs 1, 2.
The individual cementation exponent “m” ranged from 1.83 to 1.85 with an average value of 1.84 for the four [4] samples.

The individual saturation exponent “n” ranged from 1.87 to 1.90 with an average value of 1.88 for the four [4] samples.

• Cation Exchange Capacity:
    Table 4.
The cation exchange capacity ranged from 1.0800 to 1.1600, with an average of 1.1150 meq / 100 gr, for the four [4] samples analyzed.

• Air-Brine Drainage Capillary Pressure by Centrifuge Method at NOB Conditions:
    Table 3, Graph 3.
An irreducible water saturation of 83.7% of pore volume was measured at the end of drainage testing for the sample at 3184 m.

Water saturation measurements could not be made on three [3] of the samples which were very tight.

• Mercury Injection Capillary Pressure and Pore Size Distribution to Maximum of 2,000 psi :
    Figure 3, Tables 5a-d, Graphs 4a-d, 5a-d, 6a-d.
The pore size distributions for the samples were calculated by dividing the range of pore throat sizes [r] into three [3] primary groups, with an average of macro 15.8%, meso 1.0% and micro 83.2%.

c. Laboratory Procedures

The following are generalized procedures involved in the measurements provided.

Routine core analysis measurements used in this report are those described in earlier report by UZMA/PTG.

• Samples

Four [4] rotary sidewall core samples were selected for the SCAL measurements requested by PCSB.

• Fluid Preparation

Synthetic formation brine [3,500 ppm] was prepared, as requested by PCSB, using regent grade chemicals and de-ionized water. The fluid was evacuated of air and pre filtered through a 0.45 micron millipore™ prior to use.

• Sample Saturation

The samples were initially loaded into a saturation cell and evacuated for a minimum of 24 hours. The cell was filled with simple formation brine 3,500 NaCl ppm. The pressure was increased to 2,000 psig and maintained for a minimum of two [2] hours. The saturated samples were removed from the cell and weighed, and the gravimetric saturated pore volume was calculated and compared to the gas expansion pore volume to verify complete saturation.

• Formation Resistivity Factor at Net Overburden Conditions

The saturated samples were placed in individual hydrostatic core holders with a net overburden pressure of 560 psi. The downstream and upstream face of each sample was in

contact with a silver membrane and silver end-stem. The formation resistivity factor \( F_R \) at each pressure was calculated from each sample from resistance measurements of the brine and the brine saturated plugs. The Archie porosity [cementation] exponent “m” was calculated from the slope of the composite formation resistivity factor-porosity relationship, with values based on a Y-intercept value “a” of 1.00.

\[
F = \frac{a}{\phi^m}
\]

Where:
\[
\begin{align*}
F & = \text{Formation factor} \\
a & = \text{Intercept} \\
m & = \text{Porosity exponent} \\
\phi & = \text{Porosity}
\end{align*}
\]

• *Resistivity Index by Continuous Injection Method at NOB Conditions*

The formation resistivity index, \( I_R \), was measured by the continuous injection method, in which light oil is injected at very low rates. Effluent water was monitored and saturation was calculated. The Archie saturation exponent “n” was calculated from the resistivity index-saturation relationships.

\[
RI = \frac{a}{Sw^n}
\]

Where:
\[
\begin{align*}
RI & = \text{Resistivity index} \\
a & = \text{Intercept} \\
Sw & = \text{Water saturation} \\
n & = \text{Saturation exponent}
\end{align*}
\]

• *Cation Exchange Capacity*

The end trim samples were cleaned using toluene and subsequently dried then crushed and ground to a size of less than the 60-mesh sieve. The powder was then measure cation exchange capacity on each sample by standard titration method.

The value of Qv was calculated using the following equation:

\[
Qv = \frac{\text{CEC (1-}\phi\text{) GD}}{\phi*100}
\]

Where:
\[
\begin{align*}
Qv & = \text{Quantity of clay exchangeable cation present} \\
\text{CEC} & = \text{Cation exchange capacity as meq/100 grams} \\
\phi & = \text{Porosity, fraction} \\
\text{GD} & = \text{Grain density, gr/cc}
\end{align*}
\]

• Air-Brine Capillary Pressure by Centrifuge at NOB Conditions

The saturated samples were loaded to centrifuge buckets at ambient temperature. They were spun at increasing incremental speeds equivalent to pressures of 1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 8.0, 15.0, 25.0, 50.0, 100.0 and 200psi.

Effluent brine volumes were monitored at each incremental pressure until stable. Average brine saturations were determined volumetrically then adjusted to end-face saturations using the Forbes calculations.

• Mercury Injection Capillary

One quarter [1/4] inch end trims were taken from four [4] plugs for mercury injection capillary pressure measurements.

The samples were placed in a Micromeretics Autopore IV porosimeter, under a 50 micron vacuum and injected with mercury at about 33 increasing capillary pressure steps until 2,000 psi mercury injection pressures was obtained.

Results are summarized in the summary of mercury injection capillary pressure and pore size distribution data are shown in Figure 3.
